The rise of AI in software development raises a chilling question: Will Spanish-speaking engineers be the next tech dinosaurs? ## Cursor Layoffs and GFT's Vision: Evolution or Extinction for the Hispanic Developer?

The recent wave of layoffs at Cursor, a startup promising to revolutionize coding with artificial intelligence, has ignited the debate about the future of work for Spanish-speaking software engineers. This marks a turning point for "super-developers" empowered by AI, and may signal a mass exodus to other professions.

Carlos Mattos, CTO of GFT in Mexico and Spanish-speaking countries, offers an optimistic vision. According to Mattos, AI does not replace developers, but transforms them into "super-developers."

The key skill no longer resides in merely writing code, but in the ability to communicate intentions clearly and effectively to the AI. Syntax is automated; strategy is not.

The irony is palpable: The tool that promised to democratize access to software development could polarize the labor market even further. Those who master communication with AI will be rewarded; the rest, relegated to oblivion.

This situation echoes the industrial age, where possession of machines separated the owners from the dispossessed.

## AI Adoption in Latin America: A Panorama of Opportunities and Challenges

Latin America is at a crossroads regarding AI adoption. More than 85% of companies in Latin America have incorporated AI into at least one of their processes, demonstrating widespread enthusiasm for the technology, according to **Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**.

However, the reality is more complex. Only 14% of organizations in the region have AI agent projects in operation.

This reveals a significant gap between experimentation and large-scale implementation. Latin American companies are testing AI, but few have managed to integrate it effectively into their core operations.

The problems range from a lack of adequate infrastructure, the scarcity of specialized talent, and resistance to change. Federico dos Reis, CEO of INFORM for Latin America, warns that organizations that do not advance in data and process maturity risk losing competitiveness.

In a world where AI becomes the new standard, falling behind equates to obsolescence.

## Ethics and Code Quality: Concerns in the Face of AI Advancement

Artificial intelligence is not a panacea, and its mass adoption in software development raises serious ethical and quality concerns. Gabriela Ramos, Assistant Director-General of UNESCO for Social and Human Sciences, defines AI as the ability of computers to perform activities close to human intelligence, emphasizing the need for ethical guidelines to prevent misuse (**Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**).

The question then becomes, who defines those guidelines and who enforces them? Some experts fear that the use of AI could lead to a decrease in software quality and security.

If AI models are trained with low-quality code or with inherent biases, the result will be defective and potentially dangerous software. Baldur Bjarnason questions the unconditional integration of LLMs and AI agents, pointing out ethical challenges and risks to software quality and safety (**Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**).

Code generated by AI can be efficient, but lacks the creativity and critical thinking that an experienced human engineer brings. Speed and efficiency should not come at the expense of quality and safety.

The answer, as always, lies in balance.

## Key Skills for the Future: Adapting to the Rise of "Super-Developers"

If Carlos Mattos's vision is correct, and AI is creating a new class of "super-developers", Hispanic engineers must acquire new skills to keep pace. The answer is not as simple as learning to write prompts.

Beyond the technical mastery of AI tools, future engineers will need to develop crucial soft skills, including effective communication, strategic thinking, and complex problem-solving skills. Federico dos Reis, CEO of INFORM for Latin America, warns that organizations that do not advance in data and process maturity risk losing competitiveness (**Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**).

This implies a deep understanding of data, the ability to interpret it, and the ability to use it to make informed decisions. Adaptation and a willingness to learn are key.

Engineers who cling to old ways of doing things risk becoming mere spectators of the AI revolution.

## The Future of Work: Mass Automation or Human-Machine Collaboration?

Predictions about the impact of AI on the labor market vary and are often contradictory. A study by the ILO and the WB indicates that between 26% and 38% of jobs in Latin America could be affected by AI, with 2% to 5% at risk of being fully automated (**Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**).

Mass automation is a possibility, but it is not the only one. The future of work could involve closer collaboration between humans and machines, where AI is used to enhance human capabilities rather than replace them.

Juan Lombana, a digital marketing specialist, states that more than 85% of companies in Latin America use AI in at least one process, but warns about the displacement of human tasks due to automation (**Vertexaisearch grounding E-E-A-T**). For Hispanic engineers, this means that the future is not set in stone.

They can choose to resist change and fight to keep their current jobs, or they can embrace AI and become the "super-developers" of tomorrow.

## Nuestra lectura

AI is not the enemy; it is a tool. Like any tool, it can be used for good or for bad.

While AI presents undeniable challenges for software engineers, the key lies in adaptation and the development of complementary skills that enhance human capacity rather than replace it. The future belongs to "super-developers" who know how to collaborate with AI, those who combine human ingenuity with algorithmic efficiency.

Developing communication, strategic thinking, and data management skills is not optional, it is a necessity. Becoming a "super-developer" involves mastering AI tools, but also understanding the context in which they are applied and being able to communicate that vision effectively.

In the end, AI can write the code, but it cannot create the vision.
