## El Dilema Ético de Gemini 3.1 Pro

Artificial intelligence has the potential to solve problems and provide benefits if applied ethically, according to Victoria García del Blanco, Doctor of Law and Professor at the Universidad Rey Juan Carlos de España. This potential is marred by growing concerns about biases that can infiltrate algorithms, especially in sensitive contexts like candidate selection or legal decision-making.

García del Blanco highlights that AI can offer objectivity in processes where human subjectivity often generates inequalities. The promise of an impartial evaluation is tempting. But what happens when the data that feeds these systems is contaminated with historical and social prejudices?

The answer is that AI does not eliminate the problem, but reproduces and amplifies it.

Javier Murillo, Data Scientist, Founder and Director of Metrics, agrees that AI can free humans from tedious tasks and allow them to focus on more important work. However, Murillo recognizes the alarming lack of transparency in AI research within major companies. This opacity makes it difficult to identify and correct biases, turning AI into a black box whose results, although seemingly objective, may be deeply skewed.

The danger lies in the false sense of objectivity that AI projects. An algorithm may appear neutral. But its training with partial or biased data leads to unfair results.

For example, if a personnel selection system is trained with historical data that reflects an overrepresentation of men in leadership positions, the algorithm may favor male candidates, perpetuating gender inequality. This prevents companies from accessing the diverse talent they need to innovate and compete in a global market. The lack of transparency further exacerbates this problem.

It is crucial that AI developers adopt an ethical approach from design to implementation, ensuring transparency and accountability. Society cannot afford to delegate important decisions to algorithms that reproduce and amplify existing prejudices. AI must be a tool for justice and equity, not an instrument for perpetuating discrimination.

## Estereotipos y la Diversidad Borrada: La Perspectiva Latinoamericana en IA

One of the most insidious dangers of AI lies in its ability to homogenize complex identities and reinforce stereotypes. In the case of the Latino community, AI often simplifies the rich cultural and geographic diversity into a single monolithic category: "Latino." This simplification erases the particularities of each country, region and ethnic group, perpetuating a distorted and one-dimensional image.

Can we really expect an AI to understand the differences between an Argentine and a Mexican if it has only been trained with data that groups them under the same label?

In the subreddit r/artificial, users express concern about how AI can exacerbate existing prejudices against Latinos. Some fear that algorithms, when trained with data that reflects negative stereotypes, may discriminate against people of Latino origin in areas such as employment, housing or access to services. Others point to the lack of Latino representation in AI development, which could lead to the creation of systems that do not consider the needs and perspectives of this community.

Doctor Petra Jääskeläinen, a creative AI researcher at the Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm, argues that stereotypes are more evident in models like DALL-E. She stated that DALL-E "prioritizes young adults with certain visual characteristics associated with a specific stereotype of beauty." This observation is extrapolable to other areas, where AI can reinforce ideals of beauty, intelligence or success that exclude people of Latino origin.

It is essential to address this problem from an intersectional perspective, recognizing that Latino identity intersects with other categories such as gender, race, social class and sexual orientation. AI must be able to understand and respect this complexity, avoiding generalization and simplification. To do this, it is necessary to diversify the training data, including examples that reflect the richness and variety of the Latino experience.

Additionally, it is crucial to encourage the participation of people of Latino origin in the development of AI, ensuring that their voices and perspectives are taken into account.

Artificial intelligence has the potential to be a powerful tool for inclusion and social justice. However this is only true if the problem of biases and stereotypes is addressed proactively and consciously. Otherwise, we risk creating systems that perpetuate and amplify existing inequalities.

## ¿Progreso o Peligro? El Debate Global sobre la Ética en IA

The advancement of artificial intelligence poses a global ethical dilemma. Laura Miller, founder of NextGen Ethics, warns that AI could both help and hinder progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). This ambivalence demands deep reflection on how we want AI to impact our society.

Reza Rassool, a technology sector veteran, advocates for the decentralization of AI, allowing individuals and communities to access these tools independently of large technology companies. This decentralized vision could democratize access to AI. It prevents power from being concentrated in the hands of a few, and allowing local communities to develop solutions tailored to their specific needs.

The lack of regulation is a major obstacle to ensuring that AI benefits society as a whole. Without clear and transparent rules, companies may be tempted to prioritize economic benefits over ethics. This could lead to the creation of systems that perpetuate discrimination, violate privacy or even endanger human safety.

Regulation must be flexible and adaptable. It allows innovation while protecting fundamental rights and values.

Ethics in AI is not only a matter of legal compliance, but also of social responsibility. Companies that develop and deploy AI systems must take responsibility for ensuring that they are fair, transparent and respectful of human rights. This involves investing in bias research, diversifying development teams and submitting algorithms to independent audits.

The future of AI depends on our ability to address these ethical challenges proactively and collaboratively. Experts, companies, governments and civil society must work together to create a solid ethical framework that ensures that AI is used for the common good.

## Estrategias para la Mitigación de Sesgos Algorítmicos

Mitigating algorithmic biases is a complex challenge that requires a multifaceted approach. One of the most important strategies is the diversification of training data. If the data reflects an overrepresentation of certain groups or perspectives, the algorithm will learn to perpetuate those biases.

Expanding the diversity of data, including examples of different genders, races, cultures and socioeconomic backgrounds, can help reduce the likelihood of the algorithm reproducing prejudices.

The introduction of safeguards is another crucial measure. This may involve the application of "fairness-aware machine learning" techniques. These techniques seek to minimize discrimination in the results of the algorithm.

It can also involve manual review of the results to identify and correct potential biases. The key is to establish control mechanisms that allow detecting and correcting biases before they cause damage.

An agreement signed by 250 rectors of Latin American universities reinforces the commitment to a universal ethic for artificial intelligence. Inspired by Pope Francis, it proposes a global ethic centered on human dignity, social justice and the sustainability of the planet. This commitment reflects the growing awareness of the importance of ethics in the development and implementation of AI.

Transparency is fundamental to bias mitigation. The more transparent the algorithm, the easier it will be to identify and correct biases. This involves documenting the development process, explaining how the algorithm works and revealing the training data used.

Transparency also allows civil society to monitor and critique the algorithm, ensuring that it is used fairly and responsibly.

Mitigating algorithmic biases is an ongoing process that requires a constant commitment and a learning attitude. There is no one-size-fits-all solution. It is necessary to adapt the strategies to each specific context.

However, by adopting a proactive and conscious approach, we can significantly reduce the likelihood of algorithms perpetuating discrimination and promoting a fairer and more equitable AI.
