## Risks and Contrarian Perspectives

Despite the promising outlook, there are inherent risks associated with investing in IPOs. The volatility of newly public companies can lead to significant price fluctuations, which may not align with the long-term investment goals of conservative investors. Additionally, the success of the Fast-Track IPO Index relies heavily on the performance of a relatively small number of companies, which could expose investors to concentration risk.

Moreover, market conditions can change rapidly, and an economic downturn could hinder the performance of newly public companies. According to Jane Doe, a financial strategist at Wealth Advisory, "Investors must exercise caution. The excitement surrounding IPOs often leads to inflated valuations, and not all newly public companies will succeed."
