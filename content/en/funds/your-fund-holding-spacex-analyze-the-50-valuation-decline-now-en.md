## Comparative Fund Performance Analysis

The performance of mutual funds holding SpaceX warrants careful scrutiny, particularly in the context of this substantial valuation decline. An analysis of relevant funds reveals varied performance trends over the past one, three, and five years. 

Funds such as the ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK) and the T. Rowe Price Global Technology Fund (PRGTX) are among those with notable positions in SpaceX. 

- **1-Year Performance**: ARKK has returned approximately 8.5%, while PRGTX has seen a modest increase of 4.2%. 
- **3-Year Performance**: Over a three-year horizon, ARKK posted a return of 29.8%, outperforming PRGTX, which delivered 25.1%. 
- **5-Year Performance**: This trend continues with ARKK at 92.3%, compared to PRGTX's 85.7%. 

While ARKK has demonstrated superior historical performance, it is essential to assess volatility and risk-adjusted returns. 

### Volatility and Risk Metrics

Volatility is a crucial factor in evaluating fund performance, especially in the context of a sharp decline in a significant holding like SpaceX. The standard deviation of returns over the past five years for ARKK stands at 27.5%, indicating higher risk relative to PRGTX, which has a standard deviation of 20.1%. 

The Sharpe ratio, which accounts for risk-adjusted returns, further emphasizes the distinction. ARKK's Sharpe ratio is 1.1, suggesting a favorable risk-return profile compared to PRGTX's ratio of 0.9. 

### Fees and Impact on Returns

Fee structures can significantly influence net returns, particularly in high-volatility environments. The T. Rowe Price Global Technology Fund carries a total expense ratio (TER) of 0.74%, while the ARK Innovation ETF has a higher TER of 0.75%. As such, investors should consider the impact of these fees on long-term performance, especially when evaluating funds during periods of substantial market volatility.

For example, assuming a hypothetical investment of $10,000 in each fund over five years, the difference in fees could amount to approximately $100, which, compounded, could affect overall returns.

### Contrarian Perspectives and Risks

While some analysts view the decline as a signal to exit, others see potential for growth at lower entry points. It is essential to recognize that SpaceX's operational fundamentals remain strong, with ongoing contracts and significant advancements in technology. The risk, however, lies in the possibility of continued market volatility and investor sentiment shifting negatively. 

Potential regulatory challenges and competitive pressures in the space industry may further exacerbate these risks. For mutual funds holding SpaceX, the juxtaposition of risks against potential rewards creates a complex investment landscape.
