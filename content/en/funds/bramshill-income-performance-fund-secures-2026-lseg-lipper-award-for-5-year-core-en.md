## Comparative Analysis of Funds

Examining performance metrics over various time frames reveals a compelling case for the Bramshill Income Performance Fund. 

In the last year, the fund has delivered a return of 5.0%, outperforming the average core bond fund return of 4.2%. Over three years, the fund achieved an annualized return of 7.5%, compared to its peers’ average of 6.0%. The five-year performance stands out with a return of 9.8%, significantly higher than the benchmark return of 7.3%. 

Volatility, measured by standard deviation, is another critical factor. The Bramshill Income Performance Fund exhibited a standard deviation of 3.2%, which is lower than the category average of 3.8%. This suggests that the fund has not only outperformed in returns but has also done so with reduced risk. 

The Sharpe ratio, a key measure of risk-adjusted performance, of 1.25 indicates that for every unit of risk taken, the fund has generated 1.25 units of return, further underlining its efficiency. 

From a cost perspective, the fund maintains an expense ratio of 0.75%. This is competitive when compared to the industry average of 0.85%, suggesting that investors are getting more value for their fees.

## Contrarian Angle / Risks

Despite the accolades, potential investors should consider the inherent risks associated with bond funds, particularly in a fluctuating interest rate environment. As rates rise, bond prices typically decline, which can adversely affect fund performance. 

Moreover, while the Bramshill Income Performance Fund has demonstrated resilience, the concentration in certain sectors may expose it to sector-specific risks. For instance, if economic conditions deteriorate, the credit quality of bonds within the portfolio may be compromised, leading to potential defaults.

Another factor to consider is the possibility of reduced liquidity as market conditions tighten. In such scenarios, selling bonds could become challenging, impacting the fund’s ability to meet redemption requests.

Investors should also be aware that past performance is not indicative of future results. While the fund has excelled historically, market dynamics can shift, potentially altering its trajectory.
