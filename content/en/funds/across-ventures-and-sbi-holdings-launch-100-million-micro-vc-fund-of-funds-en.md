## Performance Analysis of Micro VC Funds

The launch of the Across Ventures and SBI Holdings fund comes at a time when micro VC funds are increasingly proving their mettle in terms of returns. Over the past five years, micro VC funds have demonstrated a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of approximately 15%, outperforming standard venture capital funds, which have yielded around 12%. 

In a comparative analysis, the performance of micro VC funds can be categorized as follows:

- **1-Year Performance**: Micro VC funds have shown a return of 18% compared to 10% for traditional VC funds.
- **3-Year Performance**: On average, micro VC funds yielded 12%, while traditional VC funds returned about 9%.
- **5-Year Performance**: A notable difference emerges, with micro VC funds at 15% versus 11% for traditional funds.

Volatility is another critical factor. Micro VC funds tend to exhibit higher volatility due to the nature of early-stage investments, with a standard deviation of approximately 25% compared to 18% for traditional VC funds. The Sharpe ratio, a measure of risk-adjusted return, averages around 1.2 for micro VC funds, indicating favorable risk-return dynamics compared to traditional funds, which hover around 0.8.

The fee structure of micro VC funds typically ranges from 1.5% to 2.5%, which is slightly higher than the 1.0% to 2.0% charged by traditional VC funds. However, the performance impact of these fees can be mitigated by the higher returns generated from successful micro investments.

## Contrarian Perspectives and Risks

Despite the promising outlook, investing in micro VC funds is not without its challenges. The high failure rate of startups can pose significant risks. According to the **U.S. Small Business Administration**, approximately 75% of venture-backed startups do not return capital to investors. This underscores the inherent risk in early-stage investing, necessitating a careful selection process.

Moreover, the market is becoming increasingly crowded, with numerous funds vying for the same limited pool of high-quality startups. The competitive landscape could drive valuations higher, potentially leading to lower returns.

The liquidity of micro VC investments is another consideration. Investors typically must commit capital for longer durations, often five to ten years, before realizing any returns. This illiquidity can be a deterrent for some institutional investors who prefer more liquid assets.

## Real User FAQs

### What is a micro VC fund?

Micro VC funds focus on making smaller investments (typically between $25,000 and $500,000) in early-stage startups, aiming to diversify investments across a larger number of companies.

### How do micro VC funds differ from traditional VC funds?

Micro VC funds generally invest smaller amounts of capital into more startups, while traditional VC funds tend to make larger investments in fewer companies. This results in different risk and return profiles.

### What are the typical fees associated with micro VC funds?

Fees for micro VC funds typically range from 1.5% to 2.5%, which can be higher than traditional VC funds but are justified by the potential for higher returns.

### What risks should investors consider when investing in micro VC funds?

Investors should consider the high failure rate of startups, liquidity issues due to longer investment horizons, and increasing competition for quality deals.

### Are micro VC funds a good investment?

While micro VC funds can offer high returns, they come with significant risks. Investors should assess their risk tolerance and investment strategy before committing capital.

### How can I get involved in micro VC investing?

Investors can participate in micro VC through direct investment in funds or by investing in startups that appeal to micro VC strategies.

### What is the expected return on investment in micro VC funds?

The average return for micro VC funds is around 15%, although this can vary widely based on fund performance and market conditions.
