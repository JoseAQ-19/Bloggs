Compute anatomy plays a significant role in model training, and the contrasting designs of major players can't be ignored. This 30x jump over the previous generation is substantial, increasing the upper limit of model sizes, subsequently driving innovation.

Further down the line, the entry of Google Gemini Pro with LAT-168B has sent the industry into a frenzy. Its 190B parameter count, dwarfing the competition, might make it worth exploring if we're reaching a ceiling in the current model architecture landscape.

### The H100 Boost
NVIDIA's H100 GPU brought about a significant shift in transformer-based model performance. The 30x jump over the previous generation indicates an underlying design overhaul, the cause of this substantial growth.

Looking at the H100 from a compute anatomy perspective, the increased performance is primarily driven by advances in its HBM3 memory stacks and faster interconnects. This is crucial for the training of transformer models that often have billions of parameters.

To contextualize this development, consider the release of GPT-4o, a 70B parameter model that became possible, at least partly, due to hardware advancements like the H100.

However, the search for efficient usage of these powerful GPUs has also become increasingly important due to their power consumption.

As developers try to squeeze as much performance out of these GPUs as possible, they're met with a major challenge: balancing performance and energy consumption.

#### Getting Real About Power Consumption
Power consumption for these behemoths is a significant issue. An H100 GPU, capable of peak performance, consumes a substantial 700W.

That said, as researchers delve deeper into optimizing their models and software to run more efficiently, more workloads get squeezed into a tiny window of peak performance, letting power consumption level off.

The topic that has been inadequately discussed in the current landscape is just how sustainable these power-hungry GPUs are for the overall model training process.

More work would be needed to explore if this escalating energy demand would further limit model scalability.

Now, let's transition to the role of the H100 GPU in conjunction with emerging AI models.

### LAT-168B and Gemini Pro: Are They Too Much?
Google's Gemini Pro with LAT-168B, a gargantuan model boasting a 190B parameter count, is worth considering. Does it truly innovate in the landscape?

From an architectural perspective, Gemini Pro still clings to the familiar transformer backbone. Yet, some essential design decisions, such as cross-layer weight sharing for increased parallelization and a strong emphasis on zero-shot generalization through the latest zero-shot score, might provide distinct advantages.

To better understand the Gemini Pro model, consider its potential to tackle real-world problems and fresh input from major tech players.

Looking at the architectural design decisions taken by the LAT-168B team might shed more light on the needed advancements in the hardware space to efficiently train these model monoliths.

Their choice of considerable model size paired with tightly controlled regimes of HBM and scaling up attempts is an advancement worth watching.

The hypothetical closed-loop performance power balance offered by such a considerable model count paired with the H100 might potentially kick-start a fresh feedback loop between AI hardware innovation and software design.

This might also represent how modern AI model training provides opportunities in making transformational growth through building up radical consensus on making real the promise of substantial hardware infrastructure growth when the code gets rewritten with more AI- generalized chip's register use in sight.

Assuming that hardware growth offers an answer, significant qualitative innovation and growth in AI modeling might yet again drive transformational hardware infrastructural advancements.

On this basis, it could become clear that this closed-loop design can operate this forward and backing power interplay between the AI space and the technology that supports it.

### AI's Carbon Footprint
With bigger models come more resource-intensive training processes. As this trend continues, we must start questioning the environmental implications of these activities.

Power consumption and its side effects on our planet are becoming increasingly recognized issues within the tech community.

Model training is already known to have an enormous impact on the environment due to carbon emissions.

To contextualize, the entire country of Belgium uses less power than all the GPUs used for model training in a given year.

Because of this, the onus falls on both software architects to plan for power efficiency and hardware manufacturers to keep pushing the boundaries of power efficiency.

Stealth fundraisers of startups and grinders of deep learning and GPU "HOGS" aren't being called out for their impact, yet we should do better in demonstrating our concern for sustainability.
