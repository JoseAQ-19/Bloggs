## The SEC's Retreat: Why Lighter Regulation Doesn't Equal Safe Investment

The Securities and Exchange Commission initiated only 13 cryptocurrency-related actions in 2025, a dramatic 60% decrease from the 33 actions in 2024, according to data from **Cornerstone Research**. This precipitous drop in enforcement represents a fundamental shift in regulatory philosophy that extends beyond mere policy adjustment to something resembling regulatory abdication.

Monetary penalties imposed in 2025 totaled only $142 million, less than 3% of the 2024 penalties, creating a perverse incentive structure where the cost of non-compliance has plummeted dramatically. This drastic reduction in financial consequences means that bad actors now face minimal downside for fraudulent behavior, while legitimate market participants who follow regulatory guidelines find themselves at a competitive disadvantage to those willing to push boundaries.

The SEC's retreat appears particularly strategic given its timing, coinciding with the explosive growth of retail crypto adoption and transaction volumes. TRM **Labs** reports that stablecoins reached their highest-ever annual transaction volume, rising 83% between July 2024 and July 2025, reaching over USD 4 trillion in transaction volume between January 2025 and July 2025. This massive increase in transaction volume occurs against a backdrop of reduced oversight, creating a dangerous combination that puts retail investors at significant risk.

## Stablecoin Systemic Risk: The Achilles' Heel of Trump's Crypto Vision

Stablecoins reached their highest-ever annual transaction volume, rising 83% between July 2024 and July 2025, reaching over USD 4 trillion in transaction volume between January 2025 and July 2025, according to **TRM Labs**. This explosive growth has occurred without commensurate regulatory oversight or stress-testing, creating a potential systemic risk that could threaten broader financial stability.

The Trump administration's apparent enthusiasm for stablecoins, particularly those connected to the Trump family or their allies, ignores the fundamental reality that these instruments function effectively as private money. Without proper reserves, auditing standards, or redemption guarantees, stablecoins represent a classic recipe for financial disaster, particularly during periods of market stress when redemption pressures spike.

TRM Labs data shows that extreme concentration creates significant systemic risk that could ripple through financial markets if either company experiences operational problems or faces a sudden wave of redemptions. This concentration risk is particularly acute when the largest issuers have political connections that might insulate them from appropriate regulatory scrutiny, creating a moral hazard problem that could encourage increasingly risky behavior over time.

The intersection of stablecoin growth and regulatory retreat creates a particularly dangerous dynamic. As transaction volumes surge past $4 trillion annually, the potential for a single point of failure to trigger broad financial disruption increases exponentially. Unlike traditional financial instruments which are subject to extensive oversight and reserve requirements, stablecoins operate in a regulatory gray zone that allows for explosive growth without corresponding safety mechanisms.

Kushner's potential involvement in stablecoin ventures through Middle Eastern connections adds another layer of complexity to these systemic risks. Financial flows from potentially opaque sources in regions with different regulatory standards could introduce additional vulnerabilities into a system that is already inadequately monitored. The combination of political protection, foreign capital, and regulatory minimalism creates precisely the conditions that have historically preceded major financial crises.

The administration's failure to implement meaningful stress-testing of digital asset markets and their potential to transmit shocks during volatility represents a fundamental dereliction of regulatory responsibility. While individual investors might bear the immediate brunt of a stablecoin collapse, the interconnected nature of modern financial systems means such a failure could quickly spread to traditional banking and broader financial markets.
