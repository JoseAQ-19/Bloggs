## The $75B IPO That Left Asia Out in the Cold
SpaceX's upcoming IPO is set to raise approximately $75 billion, but underwriters have blocked orders from mainland China and Hong Kong due to compliance risks, limiting access for a significant Asian investor base. Named Entity: SpaceX. Key data point: SpaceX plans to list under the ticker SPCX on June 12, 2026, offering roughly 555.6 million shares priced at $135 each.

The regulatory restrictions on SpaceX's IPO have created a unique opportunity for crypto-based solutions to fill the gap. Asian investors are increasingly seeking alternative ways to gain exposure to SpaceX's growth, and crypto platforms are stepping up to provide them with innovative solutions.

## Crypto's Backdoor: Perpetual Futures and Tokenized Shares
To circumvent the IPO restrictions, Asian investors are turning to crypto-based solutions like perpetual futures contracts and tokenized shares. Companies like Ondo Finance and Hyperliquid are launching products that cater to this growing demand. Ondo Finance is planning to launch tokenized SPCX shares on Ethereum and Solana, accessible to non-US users through wallets like MetaMask.

Perpetual futures contracts, offered by Hyperliquid, provide traders with synthetic exposure to SpaceX's price movements without ever touching an actual share. These contracts are traded on a 24/7 basis, allowing investors to react to market fluctuations in real-time.

## Proxy Investments: How Indirect Exposure is Becoming Mainstream
Companies in the aerospace supply chain, such as Chin-Poon Industrial and Sunway Communication, have become proxy investments for those seeking exposure to SpaceX's growth. These Taiwanese suppliers offer indirect exposure to SpaceX's trajectory through their roles in the broader space sector.

The trend towards proxy investments is a reflection of the growing interest in the space industry and the increasing demand for innovative financial products that cater to this demand.

## The Risks of Perpetual Futures and Tokenized Shares
While perpetual futures and tokenized shares offer innovative solutions, they come with risks such as liquidation and dependence on the custodian's solvency. These risks are particularly relevant for cross-border investors navigating US equity laws.

Investors need to be aware of the potential risks associated with these new financial instruments and take steps to mitigate them. This includes conducting thorough research on the products and platforms, as well as ensuring that they comply with relevant regulations.
