## The $1 Stablecoin Controversy

World Liberty Financial (WLFI) recently announced a partnership with a Southeast Asian entity, AB, with the express purpose of integrating WLFI’s USD1 stablecoin into AB’s blockchain ecosystem. This endeavor comes at a time when regulatory scrutiny is intensifying, particularly following the U.S. government's crackdown on the Prince Group, a criminal organization implicated in running extensive scams that defrauded investors of billions.

The partnership seeks to enable the use of WLFI’s USD1 stablecoin on AB's blockchain network, which has raised significant questions regarding the integrity of the collaboration and the regulatory implications of associating with parties linked to criminal activities. The Prince Group is said to have orchestrated a series of scams, notably “pig butchering” schemes, which involved luring victims into investing large sums into fictitious projects. 

According to the **Wall Street Journal**, the U.S. Treasury Department sanctioned over 140 individuals and entities associated with the Prince Group, which puts WLFI's partnership with AB under a glaring spotlight. The implications of this partnership, especially in light of the recent sanctions, suggest that WLFI may be venturing into a quagmire of reputational risk and regulatory scrutiny that could severely impact its operations and investors' confidence.

## The Flawed Narrative of Due Diligence

WLFI has publicly defended its actions by asserting that their compliance checks did not reveal any concerning ties to sanctioned individuals at the time of their agreement with AB. The company's compliance team claims to have conducted thorough due diligence relevant to the transaction, insisting that no links to the sanctioned parties were discovered. However, the timing of the partnership announcement—just weeks after the U.S. government’s crackdown—raises significant concerns about their vetting processes.

According to **Crypto Times**, WLFI's legal representatives have stated that the company became aware of the allegations linking AB to the Prince Group only in January 2026, well after the partnership had been formalized. This raises questions about the efficacy of WLFI's compliance checks and the potential lack of transparency in their operational procedures. 

The assertion that due diligence was performed becomes increasingly tenuous when viewed through the lens of the broader regulatory landscape, which has grown increasingly hostile towards crypto enterprises perceived to engage in dubious partnerships. The crypto industry has been criticized for its rapid growth outpacing regulatory frameworks, leading to partnerships with entities that may not align with compliance norms.
