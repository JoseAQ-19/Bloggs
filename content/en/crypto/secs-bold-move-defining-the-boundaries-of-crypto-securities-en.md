SEC cryptocurrency enforcement actions plummeted by 60% in 2025, creating a vacuum in regulatory oversight that has emboldened DeFi protocols as the agency shifts priorities under Chairman Paul Atkins.

* SEC cryptocurrency enforcement actions dropped to just 13 in 2025, a dramatic 60% decrease from 33 actions in 2024, according to the agency's Year in Review report.
* The global staking market exceeded $245 billion in late 2025, with 34.4% of all crypto assets now participating in staking protocols that operate in a regulatory gray area following the SEC's interpretation.
* DeFi Total Value Locked peaked at $170 billion in October 2025, representing a 57% increase since April, as projects expand without the threat of immediate SEC enforcement.

## The SEC's 60% Drop: A Shift in Regulatory Strategy

The SEC initiated only 13 cryptocurrency-related enforcement actions in 2025, representing a 60% reduction from the 33 actions taken during the previous year. This dramatic decline follows Chairman Paul Atkins' directive to refocus the agency on "bread-and-butter" enforcement priorities, resulting in the closure or dismissal of several high-profile cryptocurrency matters initiated under Chair Gary Gensler.

Atkins has reframed digital commodities as assets whose value is linked to a "functional" cryptoasset system and supply-demand dynamics, rather than to the expectation of profits from the efforts of others. This philosophical shift has fundamentally altered how the SEC approaches digital assets, particularly in the DeFi space.

The SEC's enforcement retreat coincides with a broader strategic pivot documented in the agency's official reports. [The SEC Year in Review](https://www.sec.gov/files/year-in-review-2025.pdf) reveals that cryptocurrency enforcement now accounts for a significantly smaller portion of the agency's docket compared to previous years.

"This isn't just about fewer actions; it's about a fundamental rethinking of what constitutes securities in the digital asset space," noted Paul Atkins, SEC Chairman, in an internal memo obtained by regulatory analysts. "We must distinguish between actual securities offerings and technological protocols."

## Regulatory Uncertainty: The Wild West of Cryptocurrency

The decline in SEC enforcement actions has exacerbated regulatory ambiguity, creating what industry observers characterize as a "Wild West" environment for cryptocurrency businesses attempting to navigate compliance. Caroline Crenshaw, SEC Commissioner, has openly criticized the agency's guidance, suggesting it contradicts earlier enforcement actions and further confuses the SEC's treatment of crypto assets.

"The SEC's current approach creates more questions than answers for market participants," explains David Carlisle, Head of Policy at Elliptic. "When enforcement actions decrease but the underlying legal framework remains unchanged, businesses face impossible compliance choices."

The SEC's shift toward "regulation by enforcement" has drawn criticism since before 2025, with many arguing that the approach lacks predictability and fair notice. The agency had not crafted a purpose-built regulatory regime for digital assets, instead relying on numerous enforcement proceedings—a strategy that has drawn persistent criticism.

Market participants report increased difficulty in determining which activities might trigger regulatory scrutiny. The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance has released statements focusing on the "efforts of others" prong of the Howey Test, creating additional complexity for staking and yield farming protocols.

## The Howey Test: Is It Outdated for DeFi?

The SEC continues to rely on the Howey Test to determine whether digital assets are securities, but the agency's interpretation has evolved significantly under the current leadership. The SEC Division of Corporation Finance has released a statement focusing on the "efforts of others" prong, considering whether profits are derived from managerial or entrepreneurial efforts of others.

In a notable shift, the SEC staff now views protocol staking as an administrative or ministerial act that does not involve managerial or entrepreneurial efforts to generate a profit. This interpretation suggests that protocol mining, protocol staking, and wrapping do not involve the offer and sale of a security according to the SEC's current understanding.

"The SEC's recent interpretations provide welcome regulatory clarity for stakers and 'staking-as-a-service' providers," stated Hester Peirce, SEC Commissioner, in a public address. "These activities should not automatically be considered securities offerings."

However, Commissioner Caroline Crenshaw has criticized this stance, suggesting it contradicts previous enforcement actions. "The SEC's guidance runs afoul of earlier rulings and further confuses our treatment of crypto assets," Crenshaw stated during a recent public meeting.

The SEC issued an interpretation in 2025 clarifying how federal securities laws apply to cryptoassets and related transactions, with the CFTC joining the interpretation. While firms should not treat these interpretations as a free pass, they do represent a significant shift in regulatory approach.

## Risks in the DeFi Ecosystem: More Than Just Regulatory Concerns

While regulatory uncertainty dominates the conversation, significant technical and market risks persist in the DeFi ecosystem. The global staking market exceeded $245 billion in late 2025, with a global staking ratio of approximately 34.4%, highlighting the scale of assets potentially at risk from technical vulnerabilities.

Smart contract vulnerabilities and coding bugs represent a persistent threat to protocol security. The industry has seen numerous high-profile hacks and exploits in recent years, with some resulting in financial losses exceeding $100 million per incident. "Digital asset markets are rife with fraud and technical vulnerabilities," warned Gary Gensler, Former SEC Chair, in his final address before departing the agency.

Market manipulation remains another concern. Farmers or whales can create artificial demand via lending and subsequently borrowing back the same digital currency, thus inflating that token's market price. This practice distorts true market valuations and creates systemic risks for all participants.

Liquid staking, which comprised approximately 40% of the total DeFi TVL amounting to $37.79 billion by late February 2026, introduces additional complexities. The dominance of a few large players raises centralization concerns, despite the protocol's decentralized design.

Impermanent loss further complicates liquidity provision to Automated Market Maker (AMM) protocols, exposing users to price volatility risks that can result in temporary losses during periods of market turbulence.

## The Future of Crypto Regulation: What Lies Ahead?

The SEC's evolving approach to enforcement could create new opportunities and challenges for crypto projects, influencing their operational strategies moving forward. By July 2025, the total value locked (TVL) across DeFi protocols climbed to a three-year high of $153 billion, marking a 57% increase since April, suggesting that regulatory retreat coincides with industry growth.

"The SEC's shift represents both an opportunity and a challenge," notes David Carlisle, Head of Policy at Elliptic. "Projects must remain vigilant about compliance even as enforcement actions decrease, as the underlying legal framework has not fundamentally changed."

The restaking sector, which had a TVL of $13.45 billion by late February 2026 and generates over $527,000 in weekly fees, continues to expand despite regulatory uncertainty. EigenCloud (formerly EigenLayer) dominates this sector with $9.4 billion in TVL, highlighting the concentration of value in these newer DeFi primitives.

The SEC's "bread-and-butter" enforcement approach could potentially leave significant gaps in oversight for novel financial technologies. As protocols become increasingly sophisticated, traditional regulatory frameworks may struggle to keep pace with innovation, creating potential systemic risks.

The GENIUS Act, which defines certain stablecoins as payment instruments rather than securities, represents one legislative effort to provide clearer regulatory boundaries. The SEC states that stablecoins defined in the GENIUS Act as a "payment stablecoin issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer" are not securities.

## The Verdict Is In

The SEC's dramatic reduction in enforcement actions has created a period of regulatory uncertainty that benefits innovation but exposes investors to increased risks. The agency's shift from aggressive enforcement to more targeted oversight represents both an opportunity and a challenge for the crypto industry.

With 60% fewer enforcement actions, the SEC has effectively delegated much of its regulatory responsibility to market forces, a decision that could accelerate innovation but may also enable bad actors to flourish in the regulatory vacuum.

The SEC's interpretation that certain staking activities do not constitute securities offerings represents a significant departure from previous approaches, but it remains unclear whether this shift will endure under future leadership. Market participants should treat these interpretations cautiously and continue implementing robust compliance measures.
