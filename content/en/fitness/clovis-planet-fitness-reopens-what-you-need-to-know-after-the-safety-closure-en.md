## The 94% Promise: Is Planet Fitness Really Safe?

Planet Fitness positions their Clovis reopening as a safety revolution, claiming ventilation improvements will reduce infection risk by an impressive 94%. This bold assertion rests on research indicating that combining high ventilation, minimal air recirculation, air filtration, and UV sterilization can significantly reduce transmission risks. However, the gap between laboratory conditions and real-world gym environments raises critical questions about implementation efficacy.

The mechanism behind this infection reduction involves disrupting the viral transmission pathway. When SARS-CoV-2 is aerosolized, it remains suspended in air for extended periods, creating a persistent infection risk. Effective ventilation systems work by increasing the rate of air exchange, diluting viral particles below infectious concentrations, and removing them from the breathing zone. The 94% figure likely originates from studies demonstrating how proper ventilation can reduce the airborne transmission risk when implemented under ideal conditions.

Despite these promising laboratory results, Dr. Linsey Marr, an expert on ventilation and viral transmission at Virginia Tech, emphasizes that real-world implementation presents significant challenges. According to Marr, maintaining excellent ventilation requires CO2 levels below 510 ppm—a standard that becomes increasingly difficult to achieve as gym occupancy rises during peak hours. Planet Fitness's implementation includes MERV-13 filters and CO2 monitoring, but the effectiveness of these measures during high-traffic periods remains unproven in actual gym environments.

[ALJ Decision in Planet Fitness, OSHRC Docket No. 23-1328](https://www.oshrc.gov/alj-decision-in-planet-fitness-oshrc-docket-no-23-1328-becomes-a-final-order-of-the-commission/) reveals troubling patterns in the company's safety compliance that cast doubt on their ability to maintain these enhanced protocols consistently.

### The Science Behind Ventilation Claims

A comprehensive study published in Environmental Engineering Science examined the effectiveness of enhanced ventilation in reducing SARS-CoV-2 transmission in fitness centers. The research demonstrated that increasing air changes per hour (ACH) from the typical 4-6 to 6-9 could reduce the number of potentially infected people by approximately 40-60%. However, achieving the 94% reduction claimed by Planet Fitness would require ventilation rates significantly exceeding industry standards.

The implementation of MERV-13 filters represents an improvement over standard MERV-8 filters commonly found in commercial buildings. These higher-efficiency filters can capture smaller particles, including potentially virus-laden aerosols. However, filter effectiveness depends on proper installation, maintenance, and airflow design—all areas where budget-focused fitness chains often cut corners.

While Planet Fitness emphasizes their improved ventilation system, the Clovis location's temporary closure due to structural problems reveals concerning priorities in facility management. Chad McCollum, representing the City of Clovis, confirmed that the gym was closed specifically due to "a structural problem with the entryway," highlighting fundamental safety issues that predate any COVID-19 concerns.

This structural closure occurred despite Planet Fitness's extensive marketing of their enhanced safety measures. The sequence raises troubling questions: Were structural problems overlooked during the rush to implement COVID-19 safety protocols? Did the focus on ventilation improvements divert attention from basic facility maintenance? These questions become particularly relevant when examining Planet Fitness's operational history across its 2,896 locations.

Comparative analysis reveals that structural issues are not isolated to the Clovis location. The pattern suggests systemic challenges in maintaining facility standards across a rapidly expanding franchise model. When a company opens 181 new clubs in a single year while maintaining over 15,000 employees, operational consistency becomes increasingly difficult to achieve.

Planet Fitness's $1.32 billion revenue in 2025—representing 12.06% year-over-year growth—demonstrates that the company's operational decisions prioritize rapid expansion over meticulous maintenance. This growth trajectory creates inherent tensions between quality control and market expansion.

## The Mask Debate: Compliance vs. Comfort

Conflicting information regarding mask mandates at Planet Fitness locations creates significant confusion among potential members. While Chris Rondeau, CEO of Planet Fitness, states that masks are mandated at all locations in New York, reports indicate that masks may not be required during workouts in other regions. This inconsistency undermines the company's safety narrative and creates potential compliance loopholes.

The mechanism behind mask effectiveness involves reducing both the emission and inhalation of respiratory droplets. When properly fitted, masks can block approximately 70-95% of particles containing viruses, depending on the mask type and fit. However, inconsistent enforcement negates these benefits, creating false complacency among members.

This mask policy inconsistency becomes particularly problematic in light of Planet Fitness's "Crowd Meter" app feature, which allows members to check gym capacity before visiting. While the app helps manage occupancy, it fails to address the critical variable of mask compliance within the facility. Without standardized mask policies enforced across all locations, the overall safety protocol remains compromised.

### CO2 Monitoring: Effective Safety Metric or Marketing Tool?

Planet Fitness emphasizes CO2 monitoring as a key component of their safety strategy. Dr. Marr recommends maintaining CO2 levels below 600-800 ppm for acceptable ventilation and below 510 ppm for excellent conditions. However, the practical implementation raises questions about whether these metrics serve genuine safety purposes or function primarily as marketing tools.

In high-intensity fitness environments, CO2 levels can rapidly increase during peak usage hours. A gym with 100 members engaging in strenuous exercise can potentially elevate CO2 levels far beyond acceptable ranges, even with enhanced ventilation systems. This creates a scenario where monitoring provides valuable data but cannot prevent dangerous conditions from developing during peak usage periods.

The fundamental limitation of CO2 monitoring lies in its reactive nature. By the time CO2 levels indicate poor ventilation, members have already been exposed to potentially contaminated air for an extended period. True safety requires proactive ventilation strategies that maintain optimal conditions regardless of occupancy levels—a standard that exceeds typical commercial gym capabilities.

## OSHA Violations: A Cloud Over Compliance

Planet Fitness's safety claims become significantly less credible when examined through the lens of their compliance history. An OSHA inspection at a Planet Fitness location in Edwardsville, Pennsylvania resulted in a citation for obstructed egress—specifically, emergency exits blocked by equipment and storage. This violation indicates a systemic problem with safety protocol adherence that extends beyond COVID-19 concerns.

The OSHA violation reveals a pattern of prioritizing operational efficiency over safety compliance. When emergency exits are obstructed, the facility fails to meet basic safety requirements, regardless of ventilation improvements or mask policies. This fundamental compliance failure suggests that Planet Fitness's safety culture may be more concerned with appearance than substance.

[Planet Fitness's OSHA record](https://www.osha.gov/ords/imis/establishment.violation_detail?citation_id=01002B&id=1559248.015) shows multiple instances of safety violations across different locations, indicating that these issues are not isolated but represent systemic challenges in the company's operational model. When a company cannot maintain basic egress compliance, their ability to implement and maintain more complex COVID-19 safety protocols becomes highly questionable.

## The Real Impact: A Cautious Return to Fitness

For fitness enthusiasts, the Planet Fitness reopening represents a complex decision matrix. On one hand, enhanced ventilation and safety protocols offer improved protection compared to pre-pandemic conditions. On the other hand, structural issues, inconsistent mask policies, and compliance violations create legitimate safety concerns.

The mechanism of transmission in gym environments remains particularly concerning. High-intensity exercise increases both respiratory rate and aerosol production, creating perfect conditions for viral transmission. Studies have shown that individuals exercising vigorously can emit up to 132 times more aerosol particles than when at rest, dramatically increasing transmission risk even with improved ventilation.

Dr. Marr's recommendations for gym safety include maximizing airflow through doors and windows, maintaining 10+ feet of distance between people, and continuously monitoring ventilation effectiveness. However, implementing these measures in a high-traffic, budget-friendly fitness environment creates practical challenges that Planet Fitness appears ill-equipped to address.

From a public health perspective, fitness centers remain high-risk environments for SARS-CoV-2 transmission due to their characteristics: enclosed spaces, high occupancy, prolonged duration of visits, and the nature of physical activity itself. While ventilation improvements can mitigate these risks, they cannot eliminate them entirely.

## What to Do Now

For fitness enthusiasts evaluating the Planet Fitness reopening, a cautious approach combining multiple safety layers represents the most prudent strategy. While enhanced ventilation offers some protection, it should not be the sole safety consideration.

Based on current evidence, members should implement the following protocol when returning to Planet Fitness or any gym:

1. **Timing your visits**: Schedule workouts during off-peak hours when occupancy is lowest, typically weekday mornings or late evenings. This reduces both transmission risk and ventilation system strain.

2. **Mask usage**: Regardless of official policies, wear a high-quality, well-fitted N95 or KN95 mask during workouts. This provides critical protection during high aerosol production periods.

3. **Ventilation verification**: Upon arrival, check CO2 monitor readings if available. Levels below 600 ppm indicate acceptable ventilation, while levels above 1,000 ppm suggest significant ventilation problems.

4. **Distance maintenance**: Maintain maximum practical distance from other members, even if official distancing guidelines have been relaxed.

5. **Hydration strategy**: Bring your own water and minimize time spent in enclosed spaces like locker rooms or drinking fountains.

6. **Post-workout hygiene**: Wash hands thoroughly immediately after your workout, and consider showering at home rather than using gym facilities when possible.
