* Dr. Cari Levy, a professor of geriatric medicine at the University of Colorado Anschutz, recommends exercise as a key factor in prolonging life, citing "move, move, move" as the top priority.
* Research shows that some hobbies, such as learning, tennis, gardening, cooking, and strength training, can add years or even decades to one's life by reducing the risk of age-related diseases and promoting overall well-being.

As we age, our bodies undergo a range of changes that can affect our quality of life. While some people may resort to unproven and expensive remedies, the truth is that a significant portion of our aging process can be influenced by our lifestyle choices. Exercise, in particular, is a crucial factor in maintaining our physical and mental health as we age.

## The Case For Exercise

Exercise is often touted as a key component of a healthy lifestyle, and for good reason. Regular physical activity has been shown to reduce the risk of age-related diseases such as heart disease, stroke, and diabetes. In fact, a study published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine found that adults who engaged in 560-610 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity per week had a substantially lower risk of heart attack and stroke.

But exercise is not just about physical health; it also has a profound impact on our mental well-being. Regular physical activity has been shown to reduce symptoms of anxiety and depression, improve mood, and even promote cognitive function. As we age, our brains undergo a range of changes that can affect our cognitive abilities, but exercise has been shown to promote neuroplasticity and reduce the risk of age-related cognitive decline.

## The Case Against Sedentary Behavior

While exercise is a crucial component of a healthy lifestyle, sedentary behavior is a major risk factor for age-related diseases. Prolonged periods of sitting or inactivity can lead to a range of negative health outcomes, including obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease.

In fact, a study published in the Journal of the American Heart Association found that prolonged sitting was associated with a higher risk of death from all causes, including cardiovascular disease and cancer. The study also found that regular physical activity did not entirely offset the negative effects of prolonged sitting, highlighting the importance of reducing sedentary behavior in addition to engaging in regular exercise.

## The Uncomfortable Truth

While the benefits of exercise are clear, the truth is that many of us are not getting enough physical activity. According to the World Health Organization, approximately 25% of adults worldwide do not meet the recommended levels of physical activity.

Furthermore, many of us are engaging in activities that are detrimental to our health, such as prolonged sitting or inactivity. This can be due to a range of factors, including lifestyle choices, work requirements, or lack of access to physical activity opportunities.

However, the good news is that it's never too late to make a change. By incorporating more physical activity into our daily routine, we can reduce our risk of age-related diseases and promote overall well-being.

In conclusion, exercise is a crucial component of a healthy lifestyle, and it's essential that we make it a priority as we age. By reducing sedentary behavior and engaging in regular physical activity, we can promote physical and mental health, reduce the risk of age-related diseases, and improve our overall quality of life.

We can all start by making small changes to our daily routine, such as taking a short walk during our lunch break or engaging in a hobby that promotes physical activity. By taking control of our health and making exercise a priority, we can unlock the secrets to a longer, healthier life.

 Actionable Protocol:

* Aim for 560-610 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity per week
* Incorporate strength training into your routine to promote muscle mass and bone density
* Reduce sedentary behavior by taking regular breaks to move and stretch
* Engage in hobbies that promote physical activity, such as gardening or dancing
* Schedule physical activity into your daily routine, such as a short walk during your lunch break or a trip to the gym after work
