## The Toothless Enforcement of Animal Welfare Regulations

Critics argue that the enforcement of the Animal Welfare Act (AWA) has been significantly weakened, allowing influencers like Aioli to escape meaningful penalties. Joanna Makowska, director at the Animal Welfare Institute, has voiced concerns that recent judicial rulings have led to a situation where the USDA's enforcement capabilities are more symbolic than substantive. Following a pivotal Supreme Court ruling, the USDA saw an increase in official warnings as a percentage of total actions, from 66% to 91%. This shift indicates a troubling trend toward less financial accountability for animal welfare violations.

In fiscal year 2024, the USDA assessed $829,179 in civil penalties for AWA violations, but the number of actual fines issued has plummeted. The Animal Welfare Institute reported that the USDA issued only five fines after June 2024, down from 63 fines between April 2023 and June 2024. This drastic decline raises questions about the agency's commitment to enforcing animal welfare standards. 

The implications are profound. If enforcement of animal welfare laws continues to weaken, it could embolden individuals and organizations to neglect animal care, knowing that the penalties will likely be limited to warnings or minor fines. This lack of accountability undermines the very foundation of animal welfare regulations and places vulnerable animals at greater risk.
