## YouTube's Recommendation Engine: The Ethical Algorithmic Shadow of MrBeast's Success, according to [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/)

YouTube's algorithm is the invisible hand that guides viewers to content, and it plays a crucial role in shaping the success of channels like MrBeast's. This algorithm accounts for approximately 70% of a user's recommended videos, making it a powerful force in determining what content gains visibility and traction. While MrBeast has undoubtedly mastered the art of appealing to the algorithm, questions arise about the ethics of algorithmic manipulation and the authenticity of content optimized for maximum reach.

The focus on algorithm optimization can incentivize creators to prioritize metrics over genuine engagement, potentially leading to clickbait tactics and sensationalized content. Todd Beaupre of YouTube's Growth and Discovery Team noted that video recommendations are calculated the moment a user appears on YouTube, based on device, time of day, and video history. This instant personalization underscores the algorithm's sophistication and its ability to tailor content to individual viewers. However, it also raises concerns about the potential for filter bubbles and echo chambers, where users are primarily exposed to content that reinforces their existing beliefs.

Shorter videos, specifically those under 15 minutes, have demonstrated the highest view capture rate per day. This highlights the algorithm's preference for easily digestible content, potentially disadvantaging creators who produce longer, more in-depth videos. MrBeast's success can be partially attributed to his understanding of these algorithmic nuances and his ability to create content that aligns with the platform's preferences. But this success also prompts a broader discussion about the responsibility of both creators and platforms to ensure that content is not only engaging but also informative and ethical.

## The Illusion of Engagement: Inflated Views and the Perilous Algorithm

YouTube's decision to change its policy for counting Shorts views has raised concerns about the potential for inflated view counts and a skewed perception of engagement. This policy change may boost overall view numbers, but it doesn't necessarily translate into a corresponding increase in meaningful user interaction. Inflated view counts can be misleading for both creators and advertisers, potentially creating a false sense of popularity and value.

While Mario Joos, a YouTube strategist and former strategist for MrBeast, claims the most important metric for YouTube success is views, as it is the metric least easily manipulated, this may no longer be the case due to the recent changes. Views are most valuable on YouTube because videos must have a minimum of 30 seconds of watch time. This requirement incentivizes creators to produce content that can hold viewers' attention for at least that duration, but the influx of Shorts views could dilute the overall value of a view.

Brian G. Johnson, a YouTuber who focuses on metrics, emphasizes the importance of average view duration, subscriber gain, and click-through rate as key indicators of engagement. These metrics provide a more nuanced understanding of how viewers are interacting with content, and they are less susceptible to the distortions caused by inflated view counts. The focus on optimizing for views, while understandable from a growth perspective, should not come at the expense of genuine engagement and meaningful audience connection.

## Children at Risk: Algorithmic Echo Chambers and Content Exposure

The potential for algorithms to create harmful content loops is a serious concern, particularly when it comes to children's exposure to online content. Within minutes of joining social media platforms like YouTube, children can be exposed to content related to violence, misogyny, radicalization, self-harm, and eating disorders. These echo chambers can reinforce harmful beliefs and behaviors, potentially leading to negative mental health outcomes.

A regression model found that video *Duration_Min* (5.66%) is a strong creative factor and that *Title_Length* (2.91%) is more important than having a numerical hook. This data suggests that shorter videos with concise titles are more likely to capture children's attention and draw them into potentially harmful content loops. The European Commission is scrutinizing YouTube and other platforms regarding their algorithms' roles in amplifying risks related to mental health, protection of minors, electoral processes, and the spread of illegal content.

The EU Digital Services Act (DSA) poses potential penalties for non-compliance, including fines up to 1% of the company's total annual income. This regulatory pressure highlights the growing recognition of the need for greater algorithmic accountability and a more proactive approach to protecting children online. YouTube and other platforms must prioritize the development of algorithms that promote positive content and mitigate the risk of exposure to harmful material.
