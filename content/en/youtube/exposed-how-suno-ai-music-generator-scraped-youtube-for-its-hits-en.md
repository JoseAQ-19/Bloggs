## Resumen Ejecutivo

* Suno AI, una herramienta de generación de música por inteligencia artificial, fue hackeada, y el código fuente reveló que la empresa había estado raspando millones de canciones y letras de plataformas en línea, incluyendo YouTube Music, Deezer y Genius.
* El hackeo reveló que Suno había raspado más de 2 millones de clips de música de YouTube y otros sitios, lo que genera preocupaciones legales significativas sobre la violación de derechos de autor.
* La industria de la música está organizando su respuesta a las herramientas de generación de música por inteligencia artificial, y este hackeo podría ser utilizado como evidencia en futuras demandas.

The recent hacking of Suno AI, a music generation tool powered by artificial intelligence, has exposed a significant amount of information about the company's data collection practices. According to the hacker, who shared the data with 404 Media, Suno had been scraping millions of songs and lyrics from various online platforms, including YouTube Music, Deezer, and Genius.

## The $2 Million Scraping Operation Exposed

The breach revealed that Suno had been scraping decades worth of music and podcasts from the internet to train its AI tool. The hacked source code included instructions for scraping audio files from YouTube Music, Deezer, Genius, Pond5, Jamendo, Freesound, and the International Music Score Library Project (IMSLP). Additionally, the code suggested that Suno used a third-party company called Bright Data to scrape music from YouTube.

Suno had consumed 2,013,545 YouTube Music clips at the point the file was last updated. The datasets compiled by Suno included hundreds of thousands of hours of YouTube Music, thousands of hours of Deezer, Genius, IMSLP, Jamendo, and Pond5, and hundreds of hours of Freesound and MuseScore lyrics.

## The Legal Quagmire Behind Fair Use Claims

Suno has argued that it is allowed to train on copyrighted material under the fair use doctrine, a subjective carve-out of copyright law. However, the major record labels actively suing Suno argue that it is illegal under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) to deliberately circumvent YouTube's protections against data scraping.

The Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) has accused Suno of ripping songs directly from YouTube, which the hacked data confirms. The RIAA has also alleged that Suno unlawfully circumvented YouTube's copyright protections by intentionally "stream ripping" tracks from the platform.

## The Contrarian Crack: Industry Compliance vs. Innovation

While Suno has marketed itself as a tool that democratizes music creation, the leaked code suggests it has been feeding on content that creators uploaded to YouTube under completely different terms of service. This raises questions about the ethics of AI training practices and the potential consequences for copyright holders.

## Hidden Costs of Scraping: Security and Privacy Risks

The hack not only exposed Suno's scraping methods but also customer data, including emails and partial credit card information. This raises concerns about the security and privacy risks associated with AI companies that scrape large amounts of data from online platforms.

## The Aftermath: What This Means for AI and Copyright

The fallout from the hacking incident could lead to more stringent regulations on AI data sourcing, significantly affecting how AI companies operate. This could also lead to a re-evaluation of the fair use doctrine and its application to AI training practices.
