## The Algorithm Dilemma: Why YouTube Shorts Feels Rigged

The YouTube Shorts algorithm has stirred substantial controversy for favoring new content over established videos, creating a lopsided playing field for creators. As noted by **Mario Joos**, CEO of Stokes Twins, this algorithmic bias is not only detrimental to established creators but also to the quality of content on the platform. Joos stated, "I don't believe this is a creator-focused update. It feels more like a push to hit internal targets and compete with TikTok. But if that's the case, it's creating a massive overcorrection that hurts almost every creator on the platform."

The data supports Joos' concerns. YouTube Shorts is now responsible for 10% of total YouTube watch time in the U.S., a figure that reflects its growing influence on both viewer engagement and creator strategy. Since its launch, Shorts has seen explosive growth, with a staggering **200 billion daily views** reported in early 2024, up from **70 billion** just a year earlier. This rapid increase indicates that YouTube is heavily investing in the Shorts format, yet it raises critical questions about how this affects the visibility of existing content.

The prioritization of new uploads means that older Shorts can languish in obscurity, leading to a concerning trend where creators feel compelled to churn out more content to stay relevant. This not only dilutes the quality of creative output but also creates a cycle where quantity is prioritized over quality. Consequently, established creators find their older content receiving far less engagement, which can directly impact revenue streams. 

## The Flawed Narrative of Engagement

YouTube has long maintained that its algorithm aims to enhance viewer satisfaction, yet this assertion seems increasingly hollow. Creators are experiencing declining viewership for older content, contradicting the platform's claims. **Tim Chesney**, a content creator, echoes this sentiment, stating, "It pushes the creator to produce more instead of better. In the long run, if the trend continues, YouTube will become the same trash bin as TikTok."

Chesney's concerns highlight a troubling reality: the algorithm may prioritize engagement metrics that do not necessarily align with genuine viewer satisfaction. The pressure to produce content rapidly can lead to a dilution of quality, resulting in videos that may garner clicks but fail to provide meaningful engagement. Furthermore, this algorithmic manipulation raises ethical questions about the responsibilities of platforms like YouTube in moderating content quality while chasing viewer metrics.

## The Hidden Costs of Shorts

The push for constant new content comes at a significant cost to creative quality. Creators report feeling the pressure to produce more frequent uploads, leading to burnout and a decrease in content integrity. **Robert Benjamin**, a YouTube consultant, notes that creators are often left with little choice but to adapt to these algorithmic demands to remain visible. 

Monetization for Shorts is another area of concern. Creators reportedly earn as little as **$0.01 to $0.06 per 1,000 views** on Shorts, a stark contrast to the potential earnings from long-form content. This raises critical questions about the sustainability of Shorts as a revenue source for creators. As Shorts' revenue share has increased from just 4% in 2024 to 18% in 2026, it is clear that the platform is shifting its monetization strategy. However, this shift does not necessarily translate into fair compensation for creators, who may find themselves trapped in a cycle of low earnings while meeting the algorithm's demands.

## The Technical Barriers to Disabling Shorts

For users looking to regain control over their viewing experience, disabling the Shorts feed can be a challenge. However, several methods have emerged, including using the **Brave browser**, which allows users to block YouTube Shorts without extensions. Other options include browser extensions like "Untrap" and "Social Focus," which can effectively remove Shorts from the user interface.

Mobile applications such as "NoScroll," "StopScroll," and "Wall Habit" also provide alternatives for Android users looking to avoid Shorts. Additionally, the **Revanced app** offers a solution for users seeking to block Shorts. For those who prefer a more technical approach, disabling watch history can create a cleaner, algorithm-free experience devoid of Shorts. As of late 2023, YouTube itself has begun allowing users to turn off Shorts, signaling a recognition of the growing discontent among its user base regarding this feature.

While these options exist, they often require a level of technical savvy that not all users possess. The need for these workarounds underscores a fundamental issue with how YouTube has approached the Shorts feature. By emphasizing algorithmic engagement over user control, YouTube risks alienating a portion of its audience who may prefer a more curated viewing experience.

## The Long-Term Consequences: What Comes Next?

The ongoing manipulation of the Shorts algorithm raises serious concerns about the long-term viability of YouTube as a platform for diverse content. As creators feel increasingly squeezed by the demands of the algorithm, there is a risk that they may migrate to platforms that offer better monetization and creative freedom. 

The projection that the number of U.S. YouTube Shorts users will reach **192 million by 2027** suggests that the Shorts format will continue to gain traction. However, if the concerns regarding content quality and creator compensation remain unaddressed, this growth may come at the expense of long-form content and the overall diversity of the platform's offerings.

The impact of Shorts on long-form content is particularly concerning. Some creators argue that the rapid consumption of Shorts can lead to shorter attention spans among viewers, making it harder for long-form videos to thrive. This could create a vicious cycle where the demand for quick, snackable content overshadows the need for more in-depth storytelling and engagement.

## The Bottom Line

YouTube's Shorts algorithm manipulation is a significant issue that threatens content quality and creator livelihoods. The prioritization of new content over established material not only undermines the hard work of creators but also sets a troubling precedent for the platform's future. As creators continue to grapple with the pressures of algorithm-driven engagement, the need for alternative platforms or strategies becomes increasingly pressing.

Creators should explore using alternative platforms or browser settings to regain control over their content visibility. The rise of competitors and the potential for new monetization models could offer a solution for those disillusioned with YouTube's current trajectory.

If you want to escape the Shorts trap, it's time to take matters into your own hands.
