Late-Night TV's Unexpected Renaissance: Colbert Takes Aim at CBS's Legacy

Late-night television is experiencing an unexpected renaissance, and Stephen Colbert is at the forefront of this change. Colbert's surprise return to television as a guest host on Michigan public access show "Only in Monroe" has sparked a new era in late-night TV. This move comes just one day after the finale of CBS's "Late Show," which Colbert hosted for several years.

Colbert's foray into public access TV is significant, as it challenges the traditional corporate structure of late-night television. By hosting a public access show, Colbert is able to exercise more creative freedom and connect with his audience in a different way. This shift is emblematic of a larger trend in the entertainment industry, as creators and audiences alike seek out new platforms and formats for content.

Colbert's criticism of CBS during his public access show is also noteworthy. He suggested that the network's decision to cancel "The Late Show" was financially motivated, despite claims to the contrary. This criticism highlights the challenges faced by CBS's late-night legacy following the cancellation of "The Late Show."
