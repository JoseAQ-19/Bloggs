## The Uncertainty of Tipping Culture

Tipping culture within the gig economy adds another layer of complexity to the financial landscape that gig workers navigate. The phenomenon of "tip baiting," where customers reduce or remove tips after delivery, exacerbates the financial insecurity of gig workers, particularly for elderly drivers already facing economic challenges. Dr. Americus Reed, II, an expert on gratuity, argues that tipping should reflect the inherent value of the service provided rather than the customer’s affluence. Unfortunately, this ideal is often undermined by tipping practices that can leave drivers feeling undervalued and insecure.

Many gig workers report fluctuating income due to inconsistent tipping practices, which can drastically affect their overall earnings. With many drivers depending on tips to make ends meet, the variability in customer generosity creates an unpredictable income stream. The impact of this culture is particularly pronounced for older drivers like Pulley, who may rely on every dollar earned to support themselves and their families.

The uncertainty surrounding tips also raises questions about the sustainability of gig work as a primary income source for vulnerable populations. If tipping practices do not evolve to provide fair compensation for service quality, the financial stability of gig workers, especially those who are older, will remain precarious. This necessitates a reevaluation of how gig economy platforms and customers approach tipping, ensuring that it serves as a reliable supplement rather than a source of anxiety.
