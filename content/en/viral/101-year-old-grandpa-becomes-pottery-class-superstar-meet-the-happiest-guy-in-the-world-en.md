Art therapy has often been marketed primarily for its mood-enhancing properties, underplaying its profound effects on cognitive reserve and executive function. Karen Miller, PhD, Senior Director of the Brain Wellness & Lifestyle program at Pacific Neuroscience Institute, asserts that engaging in creative activities like pottery stimulates various brain regions, effectively mitigating cognitive decline. 

The current dialogue around art therapy often overlooks the critical aspect of cognitive engagement. While mood improvement is important, the benefits of creativity extend far beyond emotional well-being. Pottery, for instance, requires fine motor skills, spatial awareness, and problem-solving abilities, all of which contribute to maintaining cognitive health. Engaging in such activities fosters the brain's plasticity, encouraging new neural connections that can bolster cognitive resilience against age-related decline.

A study published in the *American Journal of Geriatric Psychiatry* highlights that seniors who participate in art therapy show improved cognitive function, with activities like pottery correlating with enhanced memory capacities and problem-solving skills. This evidence challenges the prevailing narrative that largely focuses on mood-enhancement, emphasizing the critical need for a more comprehensive understanding of art therapy’s impact on cognitive health.
