## The Crisis of Accountability in El Paso Policing

The El Paso Police Department (EPPD) has become a focal point for discussions of police accountability, particularly in light of nearly 700 complaints filed against its officers since 2018. Yet, only 35 of these complaints resulted in disciplinary action, which underscores a disturbing trend in law enforcement: a significant lack of accountability. According to the **ACLU of Texas**, this lack of accountability is not merely a matter of individual officer behavior, but rather a systemic issue embedded in the department's operational framework. 

The data reveals that from 2016 to 2021, only 34% of civilian misconduct complaints were upheld, highlighting a troubling disconnect between community expectations of accountability and the operational realities of policing in El Paso. This disconnect is exacerbated by a police union contract that many argue shields officers from critical review and disciplinary actions. The contract's provisions protect officers even in cases of serious misconduct, eroding public trust and calling into question the integrity of the disciplinary process.

The implications of this lack of accountability are multifaceted. It fosters a culture in which officers may feel emboldened to act without fear of repercussions, leading to an increase in incidents of police misconduct. Moreover, it creates a community environment where citizens may feel their grievances are dismissed or trivialized, further straining the relationship between law enforcement and the communities they serve.

Critics argue that the failure to fully implement body camera policies undermines transparency and accountability in policing. Judy Oviedo, an El Paso Police Detective, has articulated the concerns surrounding malfunctioning body cameras and their potential to hinder the accountability process. If cameras fail during critical incidents, the lack of video evidence can leave community members vulnerable and diminish the public's trust in the department.

Body cameras have been touted as a solution to improve accountability, yet their effectiveness is contingent upon consistent usage and proper functioning. The EPPD's reluctance to embrace mandatory body camera policies raises questions about their commitment to transparency. As incidents of police misconduct continue to surface, the community is left wondering whether the department is prioritizing accountability or protecting its officers from scrutiny.

This debate is not merely theoretical; it has real implications for community trust. The lack of clear policies regarding body camera usage creates a vacuum of uncertainty, where community members may feel that their safety is secondary to the department's protective measures for its officers. The cost of this lack of transparency can manifest in various ways, including increased tensions between law enforcement and the community, and a prolonged cycle of mistrust.
